    2 · Weighted sum and hard constraints

    Each cell scores S = Σ wᵢ·fᵢ with weights normalised to one. With constraints on, cells above +2 km, steeper than 15° or beyond ±50° score zero regardless of weights.

    3 · The globe

    A sphere displaced by real MOLA elevation (−8.2 km in Hellas to +21 km on Olympus Mons), lit per pixel from the same raster, and textured with the Viking Orbiter colour mosaic.

    4 · Route planner

    Multi-hazard A* over an 8-connected, longitude-wrapping grid. Each step costs distance × (1 + k · (slope + rough_pen + dust_pen)), penalising steep grades, rough sub-cell relief (>50 m) and low-TI fine dust traps (<100 tiu); slopes above the limit are impassable. The straight line is shown for comparison.

    5 · Dust-storm power model

    Daily solar energy is 3.0 kWh/m²/sol × S(φ, Lₛ) × e−0.35 (τ − 0.5), fitted so that τ ≈ 10.8 reproduces Opportunity's 2018 loss (≈ 650 → 22 Wh/sol). Battery = clamp(battery + production − load).

    6 · Season and radiation

    Seasonal insolation S(φ, Lₛ) uses Mars's 25.19° obliquity and orbital eccentricity (e = 0.0934, perihelion at Lₛ = 251°). Atmospheric column mass follows from MOLA elevation with an 11.1 km scale height (16 g/cm² at datum) and is mapped to an annual galactic-cosmic-ray dose calibrated to Curiosity's RAD measurement at Gale Crater (≈ 232 mSv/yr at −4.5 km; Hassler et al., Science 2014).

    7 · What this model does not do

    • Resolution. About 10 km per pixel: it ranks regions, not landing ellipses. Boulders and small craters need HiRISE.
    • Elevation steps. The global MOLA layer is quantised at ≈ 115 m, so slopes are regional gradients.
    • Ice coverage. SWIM maps ±60° latitude; outside that, ice is unknown and scored zero.
    • Not modelled. Local topographic shielding, thermal cycling and dust-devil cleaning of panels.
