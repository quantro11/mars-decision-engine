    How the engine decides

    1. Suitability functions (0–1) per criterion

    CriterionDataRuleWhy
    ElevationMOLA1 below −2 km → 0 above +2 kmEDL: thicker atmosphere = more deceleration (NASA HLS: ≤ +2 km)
    SlopeMOLA, 90th pct in cell1 below 5° → 0 above 15°Landing pad ≤ 5°, rover traverse ≤ 15°
    Water iceSWIM 2.0consistency −1..1 → 0..1ISRU: water, O₂, propellant
    RoughnessMOLA std-dev in cell1 below 30 m → 0 above 250 mRock / relief hazard proxy
    Latitude—(cos lat − cos 50°)/(1 − cos 50°): 1 at equator → 0 at 50°Mean insolation ∝ cos(lat); NASA HLS limit ±50°

    2. Weighted sum + hard constraints

    S = Σ wᵢ·fᵢ, weights normalised to 1. Cells with elevation > +2 km, slope > 15° or |latitude| > 50° are vetoed (S = 0) when hard constraints are on.

    3. Candidates

    The map is aggregated into 2°×2° blocks; the five best blocks at least 800 km apart are reported and named after the nearest known region.

    4. Route planner

    A* on the slope grid, 8-connected, longitude-wrapping. Edge cost = distance × (1 + k·slope); slopes above the limit are impassable. Compared against the straight line.

    5. Dust-storm model

    Daily solar energy = 3.0 kWh/m²/sol × cos(lat) × e−0.35(τ−0.5). The τ-factor is an empirical fit reproducing Opportunity's 2018 drop (≈650 → ≈22 Wh/sol at τ≈10.8). Battery = clamp(battery + production − consumption).

    Limitations (stated on purpose)

    • Grid is ≈10 km/px; local hazards (boulders, craters < 5 km) are not resolved. Elevation is quantised to ≈115 m levels (8-bit Trek product).
    • SWIM covers ±60° latitude only; outside it ice is "unknown" and scored 0.
    • Radiation, seasonal insolation, dust-devil cleaning and thermal cycling are not modelled.
    • Thermal-inertia / dust-cover index layers (TES) are not included in this build; roughness is a partial proxy.
