    How the engine decides

    2 · Weighted sum or geometric mean

    By default each cell scores S = Σ wᵢ·fᵢ with weights normalised to one. With constraints on, cells above +2 km, steeper than the model's 15° planning boundary or beyond ±50° score zero regardless of weights. Optional No compensation switches to a weighted geometric mean so a near-zero criterion cannot be bought back by others.

    3 · The globe

    A sphere displaced by real MOLA elevation (−8.2 km in Hellas to +21 km on Olympus Mons), lit per pixel from the same raster, and textured with the Viking Orbiter colour mosaic against a procedural galactic starfield.

    4 · Route planner & Mode Separation

    Multi-hazard A* over an 8-connected, longitude-wrapping grid. Each step costs distance × (1 + k · (slope + rough_pen + dust_pen)), penalising steep grades, rough sub-cell relief (>50 m) and low-TI fine dust traps (<100 tiu). Pressurized Rover mode (~20 km/sol) places terrain rest stops ≥200 km apart; Foot EVA / Marswalk mode (2.4 km/h nominal) disables 200 km rover stops and models 1.5 km micro-rests, CO₂ RCA scrubber saturation, and a protected 25% return reserve. EVA uses the Lunar xEMU reference architecture extrapolated to Mars 0.38g / 6–10 mbar CO₂ (not flight-certified Mars hardware).

    5 · Dust-storm power & live telemetry models

    Daily solar energy is 3.0 kWh/m²/sol × S(φ, Lₛ) × e−0.35 (τ − 0.5) calibrated so τ ≈ 10.8 reproduces Opportunity's 2018 loss. REMS/MEDA/InSight weather cards state station coordinates, Sol, UTC date, and hydrostatic elevation extrapolation. NASA DONKI alerts model 1.524 AU propagation & transit delay separately from instantaneous surface radiation.

    6 · Season, radiation & Monte Carlo weight sensitivity

    Seasonal insolation S(φ, Lₛ) uses Mars's 25.19° obliquity and orbital eccentricity (e = 0.0934, perihelion at Lₛ = 251°). Atmospheric column mass follows from MOLA elevation (16.4 g/cm² at datum, 11.1 km scale height) for chronic GCR dose (Gale Crater Curiosity RAD 0.64 mSv/day ± 18.8% uncertainty band) and a design-basis August-1972-class SPE assumption (λ = 15 g/cm²). The Monte Carlo test perturbs every weight 100 times and reports 95% Wilson binomial confidence intervals on Top-5 stability.

    7 · Global (~5 km/px) vs. Local HiRISE (1.0–2.0 m/post) & Data Traceability

    • Scale & HiRISE DTM Provenance. Phase 1 global layers (~5 km/px MOLA, ~10 km/px TES, SWIM 2.0) cannot resolve sub-hectometer boulders or craters. Foot EVA routing requires HiRISE stereo DTMs (e.g., DTEEC_068393_1985_068973_1985_A01, SOCET SET processing, 1.0–2.0 m/post spacing, <3% global Mars coverage); outside archival footprints, Module III models synthetic 1 m/px roughness seeded by orbital priors.
    • Live Map Building (Phase 2). Upon landing, the xGDS + Perseverance (TRN + AutoNav + MGL) engine assimilates local surface telemetry across 5 domains to detect orbital-vs-in-situ hazards (including dynamic rockfall anomalies), trigger automatic A* rerouting with explicit rationale, and track PLSS O₂, cooling H₂O, CO₂ RCA capacity, and return reserve.
    • Caves & Ice Boundaries. MGC³ cave entries are unconfirmed orbital thermal/optical anomalies (Cushing et al. 2015), not verified shelter tunnels. SWIM maps ±60° consistency only; outside ±60° is labeled NO_DATA (distinct from ice-free).

    8 · Independent Orbital & EVA Validation + Terrestrial Portability

    • Orbital benchmark (9 landers + 39 HLS-2015 zones). Against 200 random-longitude draws per ±5° latitude band, historical Mars landers rank at the 90th percentile (p < 0.005, weighted sum; 89th geometric mean) and all 39 NASA HLS-2015 Exploration Zones rank at the 71st percentile (p < 0.005).
    • Independent EVA Scenario Validation (Test 3). Because orbital landing suitability does not prove footpath safety, EVA routing and PLSS math are tested separately across 4 terrain biomes (Jezero Delta, Gale Boxwork, Nili Fossae, Arcadia Planitia) for obstacle detouring, dynamic hazard rerouting, slope bounds, and ≥25% return reserve protection.

    9 · Tactical EVA 16 km² Blend Method, Vehicle Config & Provenance

    • Local Blend Formula (Step 1 + Step 2). Each 4 km × 4 km (40×40, 100 m/cell) box fills a baseline environment state at the input center coordinate using the user's Earth date (Allison & McEwen 2000 Mars24 Lₛ), local solar time, dust τ, solar cycle, SPE flag, vehicle class, and storm forecast STORM.predictDustML (60 s scalar forecast, not a spatial raster). Each cell then blends center state and finest available orbital data via V_cell = (1 − w) · V_center + w · V_local, where w ∈ [0, 1] is controlled by the User conditions ↔ Orbital data slider (default w = 0.50 in web/tactical_config.json).
    • Finest Public Orbital Sources & Dates. Pre-cut offline bundles (4 preset sites) and live ONLINE-ONLY queries use: thm_ti_100m_32bit (100 m THEMIS TI, 2014-05-30), mola_hrsc_blend_topo_v2 & slope_v2 (200 m USGS/MOLA-HRSC, 2018-01-31; plus 2020-08 HiRISE Jezero DTM inside Jezero footprint), Caltech Murray Lab OnMars/CTX mosaic (~5–6 m/px native, 20 m/px display, 2023-04), TES_dust_index_16ppd_numeric (~3.7 km, 1999–2001 Ruff & Christensen 2002), omega_albedo_r1080 + TES_Lambert_Albedo (2001–2010), GRS_K/Th (~550 km Odyssey GRS, Boynton 2007 — displayed as coarse composition only, unscored), and USGS SIM 3292 geology (Tanaka et al. 2014, ~1:20M). Coarse layers (>500 m) display a hatched low-resolution overlay toggle and explicit resolution labels.
    • Vehicle Config & Constraints. All vehicle masses, speeds, slope limits, power architectures, sinkage/speed coefficients, and SIM 3292 unit weights live in web/vehicles.json and web/tactical_config.json with explicit source and verified flags. Unverified parameters show ⚠; every Manned rover parameter is tagged CONCEPT – not flight data.
